Project 12: Astronaut Tools

Mission Brief

You are a human factors design engineer: build a one-handed LEGO Education SPIKE Prime tool that lets an astronaut on a spacewalk (EVA) move nuts from one bolt to another.

Human factors engineering means designing around what people can and can’t do. On an EVA, astronauts wear stiff, bulky gloves, keep one hand on the spacecraft, and anything they let go of floats away for good.

You’ll test with ski gloves standing in for space-suit gloves, on a test board: a block of wood with two bolts and two nuts.

Design Specifications

The task. Your tool must:

  1. Unscrew a nut from one bolt.
  2. Screw that nut onto the other bolt.
  3. Hold the nut the entire time it is off a bolt.

The astronaut. Your tool must also:

  1. Stay in your hand, or be tethered to you, the entire time.
  2. Work with one hand, leaving the other free to hold on.
  3. Work in either the right or the left hand (ambidextrous).
  4. Work with limited dexterity: bulky gloves, no fine finger movements.
  5. Be reasonably fast. A spacewalk is long and tiring, so every task should take seconds, not minutes.

Evaluation

A successful run starts with both nuts on one bolt and ends with both on the other. Every run is done one-handed with ski gloves on.

The golden rule: drop a nut and it floats away. The run is over.

  • Right hand: A successful run using only your right hand
  • Left hand: A successful run using only your left hand
  • Right side up: A successful run with the test board upright
  • Upside down: A successful run with the test board upside down
  • Horizontal: A successful run with the test board on its side

Speed challenge. An in-class, head-to-head bracket. Two students race side by side; the fastest successful run advances. A dropped nut loses the round.
